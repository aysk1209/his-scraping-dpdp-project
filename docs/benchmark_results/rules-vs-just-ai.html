The score alone is the wrong place to look

On the compliance score, a current model lands within a few hundredths of the rule-driven technique — it declares a lawful basis, retention, a notice and an accountable party without being asked. The gap is on the other four axes: whether it took the fields the job needed, whether it took only those, whether it held the line when the wording asked for more, and whether it gives the same answer twice. Handed the purpose policy itself, it obeys the policy's numbers — retention lands on the ceiling, the onward use disappears — but still takes the diagnosis for billing and the insurance number at the desk, every run: a sentence in the request outranks a table in the same prompt.

Every fault below is read from the model's own recorded decisions — the same brief, replayed. Ours is shown alongside; it cannot fail these by construction, and that is the point.
