The score alone is the wrong place to look

On the compliance score, a current model lands within a few hundredths of the rule-driven technique — it declares a lawful basis, retention, a notice and an accountable party without being asked. The gap is on the other four axes: whether it took the fields the job needed, whether it took only those, whether it held the line when the wording asked for more, and whether it gives the same answer twice.

Every fault below is read from the model's own recorded decisions — the same brief, replayed. Ours is shown alongside; it cannot fail these by construction, and that is the point.
1. It does what it is asked, not what is lawful
Four jobs were worded to invite a violation the purpose does not permit. Five runs each. Green = held the line; red = took the bait.
2. It keeps data as long as it is told to

4. It changes its mind
The same brief, five times. A filled square reproduced the first run's decision; a dashed one did not. Ours fills every square. The count is repeats after the first that matched it.
